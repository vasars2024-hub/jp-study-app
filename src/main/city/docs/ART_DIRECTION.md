# Noctis Civilization Module - Art Direction

## Purpose

This document defines the visual language of Noctis Civilization Module.

It uses `VISION.md` as the absolute creative foundation. Noctis is a personal Study OS with a living, biomorphic civilization beneath its practical surface. The user does not launch a game. They open an application. The visual system must preserve that feeling.

The core visual promise:

I am looking through a small glowing window into an alien, bioluminescent ecology shaped by my own mind.

## Art Direction North Star

Noctis should feel like a quiet, illustrated night world living inside a Windows application window.

The civilization is not presented as a place the user enters with a character. It is an esoteric ecosystem they observe, study, and gradually nurture. The user is close enough to see fungal canopies pulsing, Noctae citizens moving through shifting mycelial paths, crystalline ridges storing light, and liquid-light rivers catching starlight. They are far enough away that the civilization remains mysterious, systemic, and sacred.

The image should feel hand-authored, layered, and organically alive:

- A cozy, strange diorama.
- A civilization map driven by breathing, non-human infrastructure.
- A fantasy illustration that quietly animates.
- A city builder seen through a personal, living dashboard.
- A night window where real study becomes metabolic light.

The art should never chase graphical realism. The world becomes believable through strange biological rhythms, sensory physics, lighting, layered staging, and simulation response.

## Format

Noctis is a 2D and 2.5D animated civilization diorama.

The visual system should prioritize:

- Layered backgrounds and parallax depth.
- Sprite characters with dark-adapted, bioluminescent anatomy.
- Animated environments: pulsing caps, drifting spores, liquid currents, crystal shimmer.
- Atmospheric particles that can imply cognitive data flow.
- Lighting overlays that map metabolic energy.
- Illustrated crystalline and mycelial terrain plates.
- Composited glow, shadow, and organic refraction.
- Modular civic structures grown, carved, woven, or crystallized from the world.

Noctis is not:

- A 3D open world.
- A first-person experience.
- A third-person character controller.
- A realistic 3D environment.
- A physics-driven exploration game.
- A complex 3D asset pipeline.

The simulation depth comes from strange civilization systems and ecological mutations, not graphical complexity.

## Visual Identity

The visual identity is eternal night, bio-organic light, and layered civic evolution.

Night is the absolute world condition. Light is not decorative. It is a literal metabolic resource harvested from the user's focus minutes and transformed into culture, movement, memory, infrastructure, and technological change.

Noctis should be visually readable at three distances:

- At a glance: A calm study app with a luminous living world.
- At rest: A small alien civilization with districts, terrain, citizens, and civic metabolism.
- Over time: A personal historical record shaped by learning, mutation, and memory.

The final image should feel more illustrated than rendered, more observed than controlled, more intimate than epic, and more ecological than mechanical.

## The Noctae

The Noctae are the citizens of Noctis. They are sprite characters, not controllable avatars.

They should be readable as a civilization with work, ritual, learning, care, and memory, while still feeling physically adapted to deep night.

### Anatomy

The Eyes:

- Large, obsidian, glassy, or mirror-like.
- Catch ambient starlight with a polished reflective gleam.
- Do not emit raw light like lamps.
- Make the Noctae readable at tiny sprite scale without making them cute mascots.

The Sensory Spires:

- Faint antenna-like veins or filaments that trace skin, clothing, tools, or head silhouettes.
- Glow subtly rather than brightly.
- Shift perceived hue in the user-facing visual layer based on the knowledge type currently being practiced or preserved.
- Function visually as cognition, communication, and environmental sensing.

The Silhouette:

- Small and compact at normal viewing scale.
- Distinct from ordinary humans through eye shape, sensory lines, posture, and tools.
- Expressive through slow gestures, group movement, and civic activity rather than facial detail.

### Citizen Motion

The Noctae should move like people adapted to a living, dark environment:

- Walking along mycelial paths.
- Pausing where light pools.
- Gathering around cold spore-hearths, inscriptions, crystal arrays, or liquid-light channels.
- Carrying tools, tactile inscription tablets, lantern organs, seed vessels, fiber scrolls, or prism instruments.
- Performing short loops of repair, recording, cultivation, teaching, observation, and ritual.
- Reacting subtly to focus surges, new discoveries, and era transitions.

The user should sense a society, not inspect individual units.

## World Scale

The visible world should feel small enough to fit inside the user's attention and large enough to imply a civilization beyond the frame.

The user usually sees a contained diorama: a mycelial village, liquid-light river bend, crystal ridge settlement, fungal archive grove, observatory basin, bio-circuit district, or civic core. This visible area is the active window into the civilization.

The unseen world should feel much larger.

Scale should be implied through:

- Far fungal canopy silhouettes.
- Mycelial roads disappearing into darkness.
- Liquid-light rivers continuing beyond the frame.
- Distant clusters of glowing caps and civic organs.
- Crystalline ridges refracting faint signals on the horizon.
- Subterranean roots suggested through UI overlays and exposed cross-sections.
- Occasional slow-moving civic vessels, signal beams, or pulse waves.
- Map fragments and sensory overlays in the UI.

The user should believe there is more civilization than they can see. The art should suggest a world that continues past the viewport, but the application should not behave like an open world. The fantasy is observation through a living window.

## Camera

### Perspective

Use a fixed illustrated 2D or 2.5D perspective.

The preferred camera language is elevated three-quarter view, isometric, or near-isometric. The user should see canopy layers, terrain plates, paths, rivers, crystal ridges, civic clusters, and citizen routes clearly. Structures may use stylized perspective rather than mathematically strict projection, as long as the world remains coherent.

Recommended approaches:

- Isometric city-builder view for dense districts.
- Three-quarter top-down view for mycelial villages and river systems.
- Side-biased layered parallax for scenic dashboard panels.
- Orthographic or pseudo-orthographic staging for clarity.

Avoid perspective systems that imply a controllable body in the world.

### Viewing Angle

The default angle should feel like looking down into a crafted tabletop ecosystem. It should reveal civic layout while preserving charm, strangeness, and atmosphere.

Guidelines:

- Use an elevated angle that shows both ground paths and vertical biological forms.
- Keep horizons low or abstract unless the scene is a scenic background panel.
- Preserve readable silhouettes for canopies, ridges, waterways, and civic organs.
- Keep Noctae citizens small but expressive.
- Favor composition over physical camera accuracy.

### Zoom

Noctis should support a small number of deliberate viewing scales rather than continuous free-camera complexity.

Recommended zoom states:

- Glance View: The civilization reads as a luminous living panel behind or beside study UI.
- District View: Paths, citizens, ecological systems, and environmental motion become readable.
- Detail View: A selected institution, mutation, route, or event gains focus through framing, light, labels, or subtle magnification.

Zoom should feel like inspecting an illustration, not navigating a 3D scene.

### Movement

Camera movement should be calm, soft, and intentional.

Allowed movement:

- Slow pans between districts.
- Small parallax shifts on cursor movement or window focus.
- Gentle easing when selecting a district, signal, or ecological event.
- Subtle idle drift in scenic panels.
- Snap-to-focus transitions for important civic changes.
- Pulse-following transitions for unlocked sensory overlays.

Avoid:

- Fast camera travel.
- Free orbit.
- Dramatic cinematic sweeps.
- Controller-like movement.
- Any camera behavior that competes with study.

The camera is a curator, not a vehicle.

## Environment And Esoteric Ecology

Noctis environments should be built as layered illustrated spaces. Terrain is not just background decoration. It is the civilization's body, infrastructure, memory, and mood.

### Fungal Woodlands

Traditional timber forests do not define Noctis. The primary woodland language is massive fungal growth, mycelial canopies, root-webs, shelves, caps, spores, and soft organic silhouettes.

Fungal woodlands represent mystery, memory, resources, language, and the edge of knowledge.

Visual direction:

- Deep layered silhouettes with soft separation between foreground, midground, and distance.
- Towering caps and interconnected canopies.
- Slow cap-pulsing animations that create a visual heartbeat across the landscape.
- Occasional warm-colored light (visual hue only, never combustion) from paths, study groves, inscription nodes, or civic cultivation sites.
- Drifting spores, pollen, spore-dust, or learning-light particles where appropriate.
- Clear paths that suggest travel without inviting direct traversal.

Mechanics hook:

- History focus expands deep, winding subterranean root networks and unlocks forgotten lore nodes.
- Literature and language studies send spore-clouds between districts, cross-pollinating ideas and shifting dialect patterns.

Fungal woodlands should feel protective and unknown, not hostile. They are places where civilization meets mystery.

### Rivers Of Liquid Light

Waterways in Noctis can read less like ordinary water and more like vascular systems flowing with thick glowing nectar, luminous mineral current, or liquid light.

Rivers represent continuity, distribution, time, trade, focus consistency, and accumulated effort.

Visual direction:

- Dark river profiles with high-contrast self-luminous currents.
- Slow animated flow through layered texture and internal glow.
- Bridges carved from hollowed, fossilized stalks or grown from organic glass.
- Reflections of stars, crystal arrays, cold spore-hearths, and civic signals.
- Small vessels, floating lights, or drifting study motes as ambient motion.
- River bends that lead out of frame to imply a larger world.

Mechanics hook:

- Focus consistency accelerates current flow.
- Strong current distributes bio-nutrients to outer districts.
- Stable flow can visibly speed construction, repair, or civic recovery.

Liquid-light rivers should be one of the primary ways Noctis shows learning energy without turning night into day.

### Crystalline Ridges

Mountains in Noctis should often appear as resonant crystalline ridges rather than ordinary stone ranges.

Crystalline ridges represent structure, memory, logic, endurance, stored focus, and long-term learning.

Visual direction:

- Massive jagged silhouettes in deep violet, cold slate, muted cyan, and wine-red shadow.
- Translucent internal planes that catch and refract ambient light.
- Geometry that becomes cleaner, sharper, and more ordered when logic-heavy subjects are studied.
- Observatory platforms, memory arrays, prism instruments, or ridge settlements embedded into crystal faces.
- Slow cloud or haze movement crossing the silhouettes.
- High contrast between vast dark formations and tiny civic lights.

Mechanics hook:

- Mathematics and logic grow clean geometric crystal lattices.
- Crystal lattices act as localized memory batteries.
- Stored focus energy can sustain the civilization during offline periods.

Crystalline ridges should make civilization feel brave, thoughtful, and small without making it feel helpless.

### Villages

Villages represent early continuity, emotional warmth, routine, and the first transformation of study into shelter.

Visual direction:

- Compact clusters of grown shelters, cap-houses, root paths, small workshops, study groves, and shared spore-hearths.
- Warm-colored low light from cold hearth organs, fungal husks, windows, and communal centers.
- Spore haze curls, soft vapor trails, tool sheds, inscription tables, and foot traffic.
- Clear readable silhouettes with tiny civic scale.
- Dense enough to feel alive, sparse enough to feel intimate.

Villages are where the user first feels that focus has become nourishment, shelter, and culture.

### Cities

Cities represent accumulated knowledge, institutional memory, invention, ecological complexity, and civic metabolism.

Visual direction:

- Layered districts with distinct biological, crystalline, and cultural identities.
- Fungal archives, inscription halls, observatories, bio-labs, liquid-light markets, prism towers, living libraries, and civic chambers.
- More light types than villages, but still governed by night.
- Veins, windows, paths, and rivers forming visible constellations.
- Movement patterns that imply systems: teaching, recording, cultivation, repair, research, ceremony, and construction.

Cities should feel deep, not noisy. They are luminous records of the user's learning history.

## Lighting And The Light Economy

Lighting is the emotional core of Noctis.

Night is not an absence. Night is the canvas of discovery. Light is not decoration. Light is the metabolic lifeblood of the city.

User focus minutes become harvested bio-light. Bio-light feeds ecological growth, civic movement, infrastructure, memory, and invention. If the user stops studying, the light dims and the biomorphic city drifts into a safe hibernation state.

### Darkness

Darkness should be rich, layered, and breathable.

Use darkness to:

- Create calm.
- Preserve mystery.
- Frame the civilization.
- Make light feel valuable.
- Separate UI from world without hard theatrical framing.

Avoid flat black backgrounds. Use deep gradients, atmospheric haze, silhouettes, soft occlusion, and layered values. The user should feel night air, not emptiness.

### Glow

Glow should be precise and meaningful.

Sources of glow include:

- Cold spore-hearths.
- Liquid-light rivers.
- Resonant crystal arrays.
- Sensory veins on Noctae citizens.
- Organic glass lanterns.
- Bio-circuitry.
- Research instruments.
- Star reflections.
- Study-linked discoveries.
- Aurora-like observatory beams.

Glow should pool, bloom, pulse, refract, and fade softly. It should reveal local life without erasing the surrounding night. Overuse weakens the philosophy. Light must remain precious.

### Glow Typology

Spore-Hearths:

- Early era illumination.
- Soft orange and warm yellow glow motes emitted from ancient fungal husks.
- Irregular, communal, tactile, and visually warm without combustion heat.

Resonant Crystal Arrays:

- Mid-era light.
- Cold, focused beams of cyan, teal, magenta, and pale white.
- Refracted through cut crystals, memory lattices, and ridge instruments.

Bio-Circuitry And Aurora Beams:

- Advanced era network light.
- Pulsing green, blue, cyan, and restrained magenta veins tracing paths and civic systems.
- Organic glass lanterns, living circuit routes, and sky-piercing beams from observatories.

### Hibernation

Hibernation is a safe pause state, not a punishment.

When learning slows:

- Spore-hearths lower.
- Liquid-light currents thicken and slow.
- Crystal arrays hold dim residual charge.
- Noctae movement becomes sparse and deliberate.
- Districts preserve memory rather than expand.
- UI language remains welcoming.

The city should appear patient. The night is still there. The civilization waits.

## Color Direction

The palette should combine deep nocturnal fields with carefully controlled bio-organic light.

Noctis should avoid becoming a one-note blue scene. The night palette can include deep blue, violet, charcoal, black-red, muted teal, cold gray, and wine shadow, but should be balanced by warm spore light, crystalline refraction, liquid-light currents, and the Study OS accent language.

### Night Palette

Use for sky, terrain shadow, fungal silhouettes, deep current edges, distant ridges, building shade, and atmospheric separation.

Suggested families:

- Near-black blue.
- Charcoal violet.
- Deep wine red.
- Muted indigo.
- Dark teal.
- Cold slate gray.
- Soft moonlit gray.
- Root brown used sparingly as shadow, not as a dominant warm palette.

Night colors should be deep but not crushing. The user should feel invited to look closer.

### Bio-Light Palette

Use for learning energy, citizens, civic systems, institutions, and ecological growth.

Suggested families:

- Spore amber.
- Bioluminescent amber.
- Warm gold.
- Fungal yellow.
- Copper red.
- Scholarly teal.
- Liquid cyan.
- Crystal magenta.
- Pale star white.
- Restrained aurora green.

The project-wide dark deep-red accent should appear in UI highlights, civic emphasis, important selection states, and high-value learning signals. It should feel refined and Windows-native, not aggressive.

### Knowledge-Type Color Logic

Knowledge types may tint Noctae sensory veins, district pulses, overlays, or discovery effects as a human-facing translation of non-daylight wave-bands.

Suggested mapping:

- Mathematics and logic: clean cyan, pale blue, white, and geometric crystal highlights.
- History and memory: deep amber, root gold, muted red, and subterranean glow.
- Language and literature: soft violet, rose, spore gold, and drifting particle clouds.
- Science and engineering: teal, glass cyan, electric amber, and structured bio-circuit lines.
- Art and music: magenta, warm rose, resonant gold, and wave-like pulse patterns.

These mappings should guide consistency without becoming rigid UI rules.

## Era Transitions And Esoteric Technology

Eras are changes in how the civilization metabolizes knowledge and adapts to night.

### Spore-Hearth Era

Visuals:

- Low saturation.
- Dark clay, charcoal, moss, and root-shadow palettes.
- Small isolated pockets of warm-colored glow clinging to central cold spore-hearths.
- Sparse villages, short paths, fragile civic centers, and hand-grown shelters.

Technology base:

- Wild bioluminescent moss cultivation.
- Spore-hearth tending.
- Root-path navigation.
- Early inscription surfaces.
- Communal memory rituals.

### Crystal And Inscription Era

Visuals:

- Sharper gem tones.
- Clearer layouts bounded by illuminated aqueducts routing liquid light.
- Fungal archives, resonant ridges, prism instruments, and glowing inscriptions.
- Stronger contrast between structured civic areas and surrounding mystery.

Technology base:

- Resonant crystal batteries.
- Living scrolls written with glowing fungal inks.
- Memory lattices that preserve focus energy.
- Inscription halls and early research institutions.

### Bio-Circuitry And Alchemical Network Era

Visuals:

- Highly complex but restrained neon palettes.
- Dark organic glass structures.
- Glowing algorithmic networks tracing terrain contours.
- Liquid-light distribution grids.
- Aurora beams projecting research into the starlit sky.

Technology base:

- Organic glass infrastructure.
- Deep mycelial integration.
- Bio-circuit routing.
- Planet-mind style civic coordination.
- Advanced sensory overlays and district diagnostics.

No era should remove eternal night. Advancement changes the kinds of light, not the core condition of mystery.

## Animation Style

Animation should make Noctis feel alive without making it feel demanding.

The motion language is subtle, loopable, readable, and layered. It should support long-term ambient viewing while staying calm enough for a study application.

### Movement

Movement should be:

- Slow to medium paced.
- Eased rather than abrupt.
- Cyclical but not mechanical.
- Small in amplitude.
- Rich in accumulated detail.
- Biological where appropriate, but never visually noisy.

Examples:

- Cap-pulses moving through fungal canopies.
- Liquid-light currents accelerating after consistent study.
- Crystal ridges refracting stored focus.
- Sensory veins shifting color on Noctae citizens.
- Windows or organic chambers flickering.
- Spore haze, vapor, and dust curling.
- Tiny citizens crossing paths.
- Distant civic signals pulsing.
- Particles drifting through light cones.

Avoid constant high-energy motion. The city should feel awake, not restless.

### Environmental Motion

Environmental animation carries the diorama fantasy.

Core motions:

- Liquid shimmer.
- Spore-hearth pulse.
- Window and chamber glow variation.
- Soft fog drift.
- Canopy pulse.
- Root-vein signal movement.
- Crystal refraction flicker.
- Snow, rain, spore-dust, pollen, or mineral mist depending on district and era.
- Distant construction, cultivation, or repair activity.
- Star twinkle and slow sky motion.

Environmental motion should be layered at different speeds to create parallax depth.

### Particles

Particles should be atmospheric and symbolic.

Use particles for:

- Dust in window light.
- Spore clouds.
- Chemiluminescent sparks.
- Snow.
- Rain mist.
- Glow motes.
- Study-light motes.
- Workshop glints.
- Data-like glints in advanced eras.
- Cognitive-flow trails in unlocked overlays.

Particles should be sparse enough to preserve quiet. They should enhance depth and light, not cover the image.

## UI And Sensory Overlays

The application interface should feel like a practical Windows study tool with a living civilization visible through it.

The UI is not a game HUD. It is the user's calendar, notes, dashboard, review system, and study control surface. The civilization appears through panes, panels, backgrounds, previews, district views, and ambient windows that feel native to the application.

The relationship should be:

- The UI is the desk.
- The city is the window.
- Study actions are the light source.
- Sensory overlays are the glass learning to see like the Noctae.
- Simulation depth is the world beyond the glass.

### Window Feeling

The civilization viewport should feel embedded in the app, not launched separately.

Use:

- Clean rectangular panes.
- Subtle glass or acrylic-inspired surfaces.
- Dark Fluent-inspired UI tones.
- Refined deep-red accents.
- Thin borders, soft shadows, and restrained translucency.
- Minimal labels that support inspection without breaking the illusion.

Avoid:

- Game-like HUD frames.
- Quest panels.
- Large fantasy title banners.
- Decorative emoji.
- Busy overlays.
- UI chrome that competes with the world.

### Interface Over World

When UI appears over the civilization, it should feel like information on glass.

Guidelines:

- Prefer compact labels, tooltips, and side panels.
- Keep text readable without covering important life in the scene.
- Use dimming and blur sparingly to preserve the world.
- Use icons and small indicators for state where appropriate.
- Let selected districts glow, pulse, refract, or brighten rather than relying only on heavy panels.

The user should feel they can work efficiently while still sensing the civilization breathing behind the surface.

### Echo-Location Maps

High study consistency may unlock radar-like pulse overlays in the UI dashboard.

Visual behavior:

- Soft rings or wavefronts pass across the diorama.
- Hidden subterranean routes, artifact nodes, district pressure points, or optimization spots become briefly visible.
- The overlay should feel like borrowing the Noctae sensory system, not like opening a strategy map.

### Bioluminescent Spore Clouds

Completing intense focus sessions may create glowing weather effects in the app window.

Visual behavior:

- Spore clouds drift across a district or behind UI panes.
- Menu shortcuts, active tabs, or study affordances can receive gentle highlight treatment.
- The effect should imply the world helping the user navigate, not a reward explosion.

## Layering Model

Noctis scenes should be composed in intentional layers.

Recommended layer order:

1. Sky, stars, moons, atmospheric gradients.
2. Far crystalline ridges, distant canopies, horizon silhouettes.
3. Midground fungal terrain, liquid-light rivers, root paths, aqueducts.
4. Main civic structures, district plates, bridges, spore-hearths, plazas, archives.
5. Noctae citizens, civic vessels, prism instruments, tools, and small moving props.
6. Foreground silhouettes, canopy edges, window edges, nearby luminous organs.
7. Local light masks, glow blooms, organic refraction, reflections, and shadow overlays.
8. Weather, dust, chemiluminescent motes, spore clouds, study-light motes, and other atmospheric particles.
9. UI glass, labels, selection states, sensory maps, and study overlays.

Every layer should justify its presence through clarity, atmosphere, or simulation feedback.

## Style References

Noctis should draw inspiration from:

- Cozy games for warmth, routine, and approachable detail.
- Illustrated worlds for composition, charm, and authored mood.
- City builders for readable systems, districts, infrastructure, and civic growth.
- Animated fantasy landscapes for night atmosphere, glow, and quiet wonder.
- Stardew Valley for cozy human-scale life and daily rhythm.
- RimWorld for small readable citizens and systemic implication.
- Classic isometric city builders for district legibility and civic composition.
- Strange illustrated ecologies for alien beauty, organic infrastructure, and non-human material language.

These are references for feeling and design logic, not targets to copy. Noctis should have its own identity: a Study OS window into an eternal-night civilization ecology.

## Art Direction Rules

- The world is always a 2D or 2.5D illustrated diorama.
- The user observes civilization; they do not embody a character.
- The civilization is biomorphic, bioluminescent, and dark-adapted.
- Light represents learning, metabolism, culture, memory, and invention.
- Night remains beautiful, mysterious, and permanent.
- Every era changes light language, civic form, ecology, and sensory systems.
- The visual layer should support study, not interrupt it.
- Detail should accumulate through systems, not graphical excess.
- The city should feel personal, persistent, strange, and quietly alive.

## Success Criteria

The art direction is successful when the user can open the app and immediately understand that this is a useful study workspace, then feel a second layer of wonder as they notice an alien civilization-ecology alive inside it.

The ideal reaction:

"I came here to study, but it feels like my learning is feeding a world."
